# ArcScore: Business Audit, Competitive Landscape & Product Blueprint

_Prepared October 2026. Market figures are third-party estimates (sources inline). Treat vendor-published numbers as directional._

---

## 1. Executive summary

ArcScore is raising $4M on a $20M post-money SAFE to build an AI scoring and placement platform for college NIL. The idea is strong and the timing is good. The current public presence does not yet show either.

- **The market just changed in ArcScore's favor.** The House v. NCAA settlement created a $20.5M per-school revenue-share cap. The College Sports Commission's NIL Go clearinghouse now reviews every third-party deal of $600 or more for "valid business purpose" and "range of compensation", and the April 2026 executive order requires fair-market value. **Pricing athletes stopped being optional and became a compliance requirement.**
- **The best-known valuation index stepped away from commercial value.** On3's NIL Valuation moved to a deal and contract model on July 1, 2026 and says outright that it does not measure endorsement or marketing potential. **No one owns an independent, explainable *commercial* value for athletes.**
- **Recommended positioning:** _the independent valuation and placement engine for college NIL_. ArcScore is the scoring and evidence layer between athletes, brands, schools and the clearinghouse. It is not just one more marketplace.
- **Recommended product wedge:** a free athlete score (supply), fair-market deal files for NIL Go (urgent, compliance-driven demand), and brand placement and roster-value tools (revenue).
- **Founder edge:** a former Chief Customer Officer of Bath & Body Works brings retail customer-data discipline (segmentation, LTV, measured lift) to a market that still buys on follower counts. Make this the core of the story.

## 3. Market context (first principles)

**What actually changed in 2025–26:**

1. **Schools became payers.** The revenue-share cap was $20.5M in 2025–26 and is about $21.3M in 2026–27, growing roughly 4% a year ([Legion Report](https://legionreport.com/house-settlement-explained/)). About 328 of 365 D1 schools have opted in. Football takes an estimated 65–75% of it.
2. **Deals need evidence.** NIL Go runs three tests on deals of $600 or more: whether the payer is an associated entity, whether there is a valid business purpose, and whether pay falls within the range of compensation ([Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/newsroom/articles/nil-go-deloittes-3-step-evaluation-process-for-third-party-nil-deals)). In year one it approved about $355M and rejected about $90M. The average rejected deal (~$51.6K) is about 3.5× the average cleared deal (~$14.8K) ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/college-sports/story/_/id/49308194/nil-clearinghouse-rejected-90m-deals)).
3. **Federal pressure.** The April 3, 2026 executive order limits NIL to fair-market value with a genuine business purpose ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/04/new-executive-order-signals-heightened-federal-oversight-of-nil-and-college-sports)).
4. **Money is concentrating.** Opendorse projects $4.5B in athlete payments for 2026–27 ([FootballScoop](https://footballscoop.com/2026/06/25/opendorse-tabs-nil-market-more-than-4-billion-50-percent-increase)). Commercial NIL averages $13.5M per Power 4 school against $173K per Group of 6 school. Brands are running fewer, deeper multi-athlete programs, and retail-category deal volume is down about 9%.
5. **The supply is huge and unpriced.** There are 204,255 D1 athletes ([NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/9/15/media-center-a-record-number-of-ncaa-student-athletes-participated-in-2024-25.aspx)), and roughly 41–45 states allow high-school NIL.

**First-principles reduction.** Every NIL transaction answers three questions:

1. **What is this athlete worth?** (value)
2. **Who should pay for it?** (fit and placement)
3. **Can we prove it?** (evidence, compliance and ROI)

Incumbents each answer one of these. ArcScore can answer all three with one model, and each placement makes the next valuation better. That compounding loop is the moat.

## 5. Positioning & messaging

**Category:** NIL valuation and placement engine.
**One-liner:** _Every athlete has an arc. ArcScore measures it._
**Core idea:** two numbers. **Score** is current commercial value. **Arc** is the projected 12-month trajectory. The arc metaphor is a ball in flight: launch, trajectory, score. It is the product thesis, not decoration. Buy the rise, not the peak.

| Audience | Pain | Promise | Primary CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brands | Paying for followers, not customers; no ROI | "Find athletes your customers already follow." | Book a brand demo |
| Schools & collectives | Allocating $20M+ without an objective value; portal losses | "Manage your roster like a portfolio." | Book a school demo |
| Athletes | Don't know their worth; deals rejected | "Know your worth. Grow your arc." | Get your free score |

**Credibility pillars:** retail-grade customer data (the founder's background), explainable scores, neutrality (works alongside Teamworks and Opendorse), and readiness for NIL Go.

## 6. The ideal product

### 6.1 The ArcScore model

Six signal families, each benchmarked against comparable athletes (by sport, position, conference, market and class year):

| Factor | Inputs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | Deduplicated cross-platform audience, impressions | Bot and purchased-follower filtering is a differentiator |
| **Resonance** | Engagement quality, saves and shares, comment sentiment | Weight quality over raw likes |
| **Performance** | Position-normalized stats, strength of schedule, awards | Makes Olympic and women's sports comparable |
| **Momentum** | 30/90-day growth, media velocity, TV and postseason exposure, draft stock | This factor sets the **Arc** |
| **Market Fit** | Audience demographics, geography/DMA, category affinity | Per-brand: matched against the brand's customer file (privacy-safe) |
| **Integrity** | Brand safety scan, deliverable reliability, compliance readiness | Lowers risk for brands and schools |

Outputs: **ArcScore (0–100)**, **Arc (projected change)**, **fair-market range with comparable deals**, **per-brand Fit Score**, and **per-factor explanations**.

### 6.2 Product surfaces

1. **Athlete app (free, the supply engine).** Connect socials and see your score, a growth plan, brand matches, deal files, and payouts.
2. **Brand console (SaaS plus take rate).** Discovery by fit, campaign builder, contracts, content approvals, payouts, ROI reporting.
3. **School / collective console (enterprise SaaS).** Roster value map, revenue-share scenario modeling against the cap, retention and portal alerts, commercial NIL marketplace for athletes outside the cap.
4. **Deal File / FMV report (per-report or bundled).** Evidence for NIL Go and the executive-order tests.
5. **ArcScore API (data licensing).** For Teamworks-style platforms, agencies, collectives, media and arbitrators.

### 6.3 Business model (proposed)

| Stream | Price signal (to validate) |
|---|---|
| School / collective SaaS | $25K–$150K per year by tier (well below Altius-style advisory at ~$400K/yr) |
| Brand SaaS + placement fee | Platform subscription plus 10–15% of deal value, under Opendorse's reported ~30% |
| FMV deal files | $49–$249 per report, or included in school and brand plans |
| API / data licensing | Usage-based |

### 6.4 Moats

The data flywheel (every placement and its outcome trains the model), the methodology's credibility, network effects on the athlete side, and integrations that make ArcScore the default valuation input.

### 6.5 Risks

- Deal-data access (On3 and Opendorse hold historical deals). Mitigate with partnerships plus ArcScore's own placement data.
- Teamworks adding valuation to GM. Mitigate by integrating first and becoming its valuation input.
- Platform API limits and social-data privacy, especially for minors (high-school NIL). Need consent flows and a data policy.
- How regulators view the model. Explainability is both the product and the defense.

## 8. Roadmap: client backend & team management

Cloudflare-native so the stack stays one deployable unit:

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| API | Hono on Workers (already in `worker/index.ts`) |
| Database | D1 (SQLite) for tenants, users and deals. Move to Postgres via Hyperdrive if analytics outgrow D1. |
| Files | R2 (contracts, content approvals, deal files) |
| Jobs | Queues + Cron Triggers (social pulls, score recompute, NIL Go file generation) |
| Realtime | Durable Objects (campaign rooms, approvals, notifications) |
| Auth | Better Auth or Clerk with organizations; magic link for athletes; SSO (SAML) for schools |
| AI | Workers AI for embeddings and fit similarity; external LLM for content and sentiment analysis |

**Multi-tenant model (draft):** `organizations` (brand | school | collective | agency), `memberships` (role: owner, admin, manager, analyst, viewer; scoped to teams or sports), `teams` (e.g. Football, Olympic Sports, Brand Team East), `athletes`, `athlete_scores` (time series by factor), `campaigns`, `deals` (status, FMV range, NIL Go submission state), `deliverables`, `payouts`, `audit_log`.

**Team management features:** invites, role-based access by team or sport, approval chains (compliance officer sign-off before NIL Go submission), activity feed and audit log for compliance, shared watchlists and roster boards, seat-based billing.

**Phasing:**

1. **Weeks 0–4:** auth and organizations, athlete onboarding with social connect, score v1 (Reach, Resonance, Momentum), leads to CRM.
2. **Weeks 4–10:** brand console (discovery, campaigns), deal files v1, team roles and invites.
3. **Weeks 10–16:** school roster value and cap scenarios, approvals workflow, payouts, ArcScore API beta.
